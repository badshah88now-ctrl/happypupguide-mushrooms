Can Dogs Eat Mushrooms? Plain Shop-Bought Only — Never Wild Ones

By HappyPupGuide

Plain, shop-bought mushrooms such as white button mushrooms are generally safe for most dogs in small amounts when they are cooked and unseasoned. They are low in calories and add a little fibre and B vitamins. But the rule has a hard edge: never let your dog eat wild mushrooms, and never offer mushrooms cooked with garlic, onion, butter or rich sauces.

Wild mushrooms are the real risk. Some wild species are toxic and can cause serious liver or kidney damage, and it is very hard to tell a safe wild mushroom from a dangerous one by looks alone. If your dog eats a wild mushroom on a walk, treat it as urgent: note what it looked like, take a photo if you can, and call your vet right away rather than waiting for symptoms.

Serve mushrooms plain and cooked, in small pieces. A few small pieces mixed into a meal is plenty — mushrooms are a garnish, not a main food. Raw mushrooms are harder to digest, so cook them lightly with no oil, salt or seasoning. Never give mushroom soup, mushroom sauce or pizza toppings, which usually carry salt, garlic, onion and fat.

Watch your dog after the first taste, as with any new food. Most dogs handle a little plain mushroom easily, but a sensitive stomach may react with loose stools. If you notice vomiting, diarrhoea, drooling or unusual tiredness after mushrooms — especially wild ones — contact your vet promptly and tell them exactly what your dog ate.
